## Built 2026-08-02 — "family" stops being a thing users manage

The diagnosis: the word does **three jobs** — the everyday word ("free for
your whole family"), an audience tier (Public / **Family** / Parents only),
and a container object ("Add this baby to **The Brady Family**"). The third is
the intruder. Worse, the container isn't a family at all: membership is
family-wide, so it's the owner + co-parents + *every person who ever redeemed
an invite to any page in it* — a guest list wearing an intimate noun.

So the container stays in the schema (it's load-bearing for twins, co-parent
access and `group_targeted`) and disappears from the UI:

1. **Setup asks about people, not containers.** "Add this baby to: The Brady
   Family / A new family" → *"Who can see this page?"* → **"The same people as
   Nia Rose's page"** / **"Start fresh"**, with the carry-over spelled out
   underneath — *"Nick and 5 viewers come too"*. `/me` now returns
   `co_parent_names` + `viewer_count` per family to make that possible. Names
   are capped at two pages, then "and N other pages".
2. **"Your family" → "Co-parents"** on the account page — that section only
   ever listed co-parents.
3. **Deleting the last page takes the family with it**, reusing the rule
   `account_deletion` already encoded: hard-delete when the birth went
   completely, scrub to "Deleted" + drop memberships when it soft-deleted
   because of gift orders.
4. **The audience tier keeps "Family"** — right everyday word in that slot.
5. **"Stop following" exists at last.** `DELETE /family/{id}/membership`, plus
   a "Pages you follow" section on the account page. Owner gets a 409 (delete
   the pages instead). The action is family-wide because membership is, so the
   UI names every page it covers rather than saying "family".

**Known rough edge introduced:** a viewer who stops following their last page
has no births, and `AccountPage.jsx:291` redirects anyone with no births to
`/setup` — so they land in the create-a-baby-page wizard, which is wrong for
someone who was never a parent. The redirect predates this work (it exists to
push brand-new signups into setup) but leaving is now a legitimate way to
reach zero. Wants an empty state, or a redirect that distinguishes "never had
a page" from "just left".

**Deliberately not done:** membership is attached to the family rather than
the birth, which is *why* a viewer carries into the next baby automatically.
Moving it per-birth fixes the semantics at the root instead of the wording,
but it's a schema + permissions change with real blast radius. Decide
separately once the surface fix has been lived with.

## Open — found 2026-08-01

- **No way to remove a family.** *(Fixed — see the closing note.)* Deleting the only birth in a family leaves
  the family, its name and its full membership list behind. `AccountPage.jsx:291`
  redirects to `/setup` when you have no births, so the orphan is invisible —
  except in the setup wizard's chooser, where it reappears as "Add this baby
  to: The Brady Family", described as "Co-parents and viewers from this family
  carry over". **The risk isn't the orphan, it's the silent carry-over**: add
  a page to an old family and everyone previously invited is quietly back in.
  Nobody can leave a family either — no endpoint, no UI, for co-parents or
  viewers. `account_deletion._erase_family` already encodes the
  right rule (hard-delete the family, or scrub it to "Deleted" when a birth
  had gift orders and must soft-delete) — `DELETE /birth/{id}` just never
  calls it. See the options discussion below.

  **Closed 2026-08-06.** Both paths that could strand a family are covered:
  `delete_birth` (`births.py:198-206`, PR #53) removes the family when its
  last live birth goes, and `_erase_family` (`account_deletion.py:249-269`)
  does the same on account deletion. A half-failed setup can't leave one
  either — `_resolve_birth_family` only flushes, and `get_db` closes the
  session without committing, so family and birth live or die together. That
  left exactly one pre-fix orphan in production, `ghn Family`, created
  2026-08-01 and stranded a day before PR #53 merged; deleted by hand
  2026-08-06. Note the function name above was wrong throughout this document
  — there is no `erase_sole_parent_family`, and grepping for it finds
  nothing.

  Still open, but unreachable today: `leave_family`
  (`invitations.py:391-395`) refuses an owner on role alone — "you own these
  pages, delete them from Birth settings instead" — which is a dead end for
  an owner whose family has no pages to delete. Nothing can produce that
  state now, so this is a guard against a future path that empties a family
  without going through either cleanup, not a live bug.

## High — data integrity of the keepsake

### 1. Editing the Born milestone's time collapses labor duration to zero and leaves contractions logged after the birth

Reproduction, exactly as a parent would do it ("the baby actually came at 6, not 8"):

1. Timed two contractions at 7:57 PM and 8:00 PM.
2. Tapped **Baby Born!** at 8:07 PM.
3. Opened the Born event's **Edit** → *When it happened* → set **6:00 PM** → Save.

Result in the database:

```
birth_started_at   = 2026-07-30 22:00:00+00
birth_completed_at = 2026-07-30 22:00:00+00   ← identical
```

The commit intends to "pull `birth_started_at` back if it would otherwise be after the birth," and it does — by setting it *equal* to the completion time. Consequences:

- **Total labor = 0 minutes** in every derived stat.
- The two contraction events (23:57 and 00:00 UTC) are now **after** the birth completed. The timeline visibly reads: *contraction 8:00 PM → contraction 7:57 PM → 👶 Baby Born! 6:00 PM*. The keepsake says the baby was born and then labor continued.
- "Baby Born!" is no longer the top of the timeline — it sinks below the contractions, because ordering is by `occurred_at`. The emotional peak of the page gets buried mid-scroll.

Worth deciding what the intended invariant is: clamp the born time to be ≥ last contraction, offer to move the contractions too, or warn and let the parent choose.

### 2. The born-time edit does not invalidate cached gift renderings — the keepsake prints the pre-edit birth time

After the edit above, the page header reads **"Born Jul 30, 2026, 6:00 PM"**. Scrolling down to the Keepsake gifts section, the Birth Story Mug artwork reads:

- **"born July 30, 2026 at 8:07 pm"** — the pre-edit time
- **"10M OF LABOR"** — the pre-edit duration

`gift_artwork.py:94` derives the born time from `birth.child_dob or birth.birth_completed_at`, which is now correct, so the artwork itself isn't wrong — it's **stale**. `gift_renderings` rows for this birth are all stamped `created_at = 00:07:58` (the instant Baby Born was tapped) and were never touched by the later edit:

```
created_at                     | updated_at
2026-07-31 00:07:58.322757+00  | 2026-07-31 00:07:58.987914+00
```

So the app shows one birth time on screen and a different one on the product a relative would pay $18 to have printed. Renderings need invalidating whenever `birth_completed_at` / `child_dob` moves.

### 3. No upper bound on `occurred_at` — events can be dated into the future

I posted a note through the composer with the time set to **12/25/2026 10:00 AM** (five months out). It was accepted with no warning and rendered a `FRI, DEC 25` day header sitting permanently above everything else.

- The composer input and the Baby Born input both carry `max={toLocalInputValue()}` — advisory only, since Post is an `onClick` handler, not a validated form submit.
- The **Edit modal's** input (`Timeline.jsx:434`) has **no `max` at all** *(read from code)*, so a parent using the ordinary native picker can move any event into the future.
- `schemas.py` declares `occurred_at: Optional[datetime]` with no bound, and the route accepts it *(read from code)*. There is no server-side guard.

A future-dated event pins itself to the top of the timeline forever and distorts anything that reasons about "latest."

## Medium — the guess pool's payoff is broken for the people it's for

### 4. The exact-weight guess lost to a 2-oz-off guess

Settled pool, real numbers from the run:

| | Weight | Length | Day | Placed |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Actual | 8 lbs 2 oz | 20" | Jul 30 | — |
| Maya (Mom) | 8 lbs 4 oz | 20" | Aug 15 | 🏆 1st |
| Grandma Linda | 8 lbs 2 oz | 21" | – | 🥈 2nd |

Scoring is `|weight diff in lbs| + 0.5 × |length diff in inches|` *(read from `repositories/guesses.py:25`)*, so Maya scored 0.125 and Linda 0.5. The math is self-consistent, but the **weighting is the problem**: one inch of length costs the same as half a pound — eight ounces — of weight. Length is measured with a tape against a squirming newborn and is the least reliable number on the board; weight is the number everyone actually guesses. As it stands, nailing the weight exactly and being 1" off on length loses to being 2 oz off. Every family will read that result as wrong.

Also: Maya was crowned **date winner** with a guess 16 days off, uncontested, because she was the only person with a date guess on the board (see below). A single wildly-wrong guess probably shouldn't earn a trophy.

### 5. Viewers who join after labor starts silently can't guess the date — and then lose on it

Linda redeemed her invite after the first contraction was logged. Her guess form showed only **Weight** and **Length**; the Arrival day field was simply absent, with no explanation. The results table still renders a **Day** column, where her row shows `–`.

The backend does have good copy for this — `"Date guesses closed when labor began — nice try 😉"` *(read from `routes/engagement.py:549`)* — but the frontend removes the field rather than showing the message. So the viewer never learns why, and the missing dimension then counts against her in the standings. This is the single most likely real-world path: the family gets invited *during* labor.

### 6. A parent who sets up in the last four weeks can never edit their own guess

Maya created the page on Jul 30 with a due date of Aug 15. Birth settings told her: *"guesses lock July 18 (36 weeks)"* — twelve days in the past. Her guess, entered minutes earlier during onboarding, was immediately uneditable: the pool modal read *"Guesses are locked in — sealed until the arrival"* with no edit affordance.

Most people set this page up in the last month of pregnancy, so **most parents will hit this**. Two sub-issues:

- The settings label says *"guesses lock"*, but the actual rule is narrower: new guesses stay open until birth, only **edits** to an existing guess lock at 36 weeks *(read from `routes/engagement.py:451`)*. The copy overstates it.
- The same pool showed contradictory states by role at the same moment: Maya saw *"Guesses are locked in"* while Linda's header badge invited her to *"last call — guess before the baby comes!"*.
- Onboarding shouldn't hand someone a permanently frozen guess on the same screen where it asks them to make one. Either the lock should key off when the guess was created, or the onboarding step should be skipped/warned when the due date is already inside 36 weeks.

### 7. The oz field accepts values over 15 and silently rolls them up

In onboarding I typed **7 lbs / 20 oz** (a plausible slip). No validation, no max on the field. It was stored as `weight_lbs = 8.25` and now displays as **"8 lbs 4 oz"** — arithmetically defensible, but not what she typed, and not what she'd notice. Combined with #6, she is stuck with a guess she never made.

### 8. "Lock in my guess" doesn't actually save until you also supply a name

Clicking **Lock in my guess** in onboarding advanced to *"Add your name so the family knows whose guess this is"*. I checked the database at that point: **no row had been written**. The guess only persists after **Save**. Anyone who reads "Lock in my guess" as done and closes the tab loses it silently. Either save on the first tap and attach the name after, or don't call the first button "lock in."

## Medium — trust, privacy, and provenance

### 9. Audience scope can't be changed after posting

The Edit modal exposes body text and *When it happened* — but not the audience. A parent who posts something to **Public** that they meant for **Parents only** (or vice versa) has one remedy: delete and re-post, losing the reactions and comments. Given that the composer's three-way picker is the product's main privacy control, "I picked the wrong one" is a predictable mistake with no recovery.

### 10. Nothing on the page indicates a time was set or changed

`posted_at` and `occurred_at` are both stored (verified in the DB — my backdated event has `occurred_at 09:30`, `posted_at 00:05`), but no surface ever shows the difference. A viewer cannot tell an event timed live from one backdated hours later, and an edited timestamp leaves no trace at all. For something sold as a permanent record, a quiet *"edited"* or *"posted at 7:04 PM"* affordance seems worth it — the data is already there.

### 11. Backdated posts arrive silently for viewers watching live

With Linda's page open, Maya posted an event dated 5:30 AM. SSE delivered it and the timeline **inserted it in correct chronological position** — that part works well. But it landed several screens down with no "new" indicator. A viewer watching the top of the feed has no idea anything was added. As backdating becomes common (which is the point of the feature), viewers will miss posts.

### 12. Anonymous visitors see a red "Reconnecting…" forever

Hitting `/b/nia-rose` with no session gives a nicely-designed blurred teaser and a **"Sign in to follow along"** CTA. Directly under the baby's name, though, sits a red dot and **"Reconnecting…"** — still there after 10+ seconds, permanently. The backend log shows `GET /b/nia-rose/stream 200 OK`, so the connection isn't even failing; the client just never reaches a connected state without auth.

This is the first thing every forwarded-link recipient sees. It reads as "this site is broken" at the exact moment you're asking a grandparent to trust it with a sign-in.

### 13. The composer promises public visibility the app doesn't grant

Selecting **Public** shows the helper text *"Anyone with the link can see"*. That isn't true: `GET /b/{slug}/timeline` returns **401** to anonymous callers (verified directly), by deliberate design — `routes/deps.py:104` documents the 2026-07-23 decision that the timeline requires a session. Anyone with the link must sign in first.

Two follow-ons:
- The three tiers effectively collapse to two for anyone outside the family, since `public` and `group_targeted` both require an account.
- `README.md` is now stale on this: the API section says public `/b/{slug}` routes are unauthenticated, and the audience-scope table still claims `public` = visible to Anonymous.

## Low — polish, and the first five minutes

### 14. Chosen theme doesn't carry through the app

Picking **Starry Night** themes the birth page beautifully, but Lily's purple/pink leaks through everywhere else:

- Composer type buttons render as a rainbow (purple Photo, indigo Video, blue Note, gold Milestone, crimson Voice Memo) that matches no theme.
- **Post** and **Send** buttons are magenta on the navy page.
- Stats numbers are pink and blue.
- Setup step 2 (sign-in) applies the gold accent to a light lavender background — white text on tan, poor contrast — while step 3 (the pool) reverts to purple entirely.

### 15. Setup wizard loses everything on refresh

Name, due date, and theme are held in memory only. A refresh (or any detour) mid-setup drops you back to an empty step 1. On a phone, mid-labor, an accidental reload is cheap; re-entering isn't.

### 16. The invite page — the thing you send to family — has rough edges

- Missing space in the headline: **"WelcomeNia Rose's birth"** (script font, clearly visible).
- Auto-generated family name reads oddly: *"Nia Rose Family invited you to…"* — the baby's name isn't the family's name, and it doesn't say who actually invited them.
- The page is light-themed with no trace of the page's chosen theme, so there's no visual continuity into what you're joining.
- It's a hard auth wall: nothing about the baby is visible before signing in, even though `/b/{slug}` has a perfectly good anonymous teaser for exactly this purpose. Sending grandma to the teaser first would convert better than sending her to a sign-in form.

### 17. Failed OTP send leaves the user stuck, though a valid code exists

When `POST /auth/request` fails, the UI shows *"We couldn't send your code — try again in a minute"* and stays on the email step. But the challenge row **is** committed before the send attempt (`auth.py:253`), so a valid 6-digit code exists for 30 minutes. If delivery was merely slow rather than failed — or succeeded on a retry that the UI treated as a failure — the user has a working code and nowhere to type it. An "I already have a code" path would rescue that case.

### 18. Smaller items

- **Settings opens mid-page.** Navigating from the birth page to Birth settings preserves the scroll offset, landing you in the middle of the Gift deliveries form. No scroll restoration on route change.
- **Contractions have no Edit.** Every other event type offers Edit; contractions offer only ignore-interval and delete. Intentional per the commit (durations derive from their times), but it means the *most* timing-sensitive events are the only ones a parent can't correct — and there's no explanation in the UI, just a missing link.
- **Setup accessibility.** The six theme cards are `<button>`s with no accessible name (empty in the a11y tree), and the baby-name field has a placeholder but no label.
- **Onboarding's preview card shows fake activity.** After "Nia Rose's page is live 🎉" the mock still shows *"Contraction in progress · 0:42"* and reaction counts 14/8/5, which reads as if her brand-new page already has traffic.
- **"Boy or girl?" defaults to "We already know."** Pre-selected without being chosen, which silently disables the gender pool for everyone who never opens settings.
- **Photos upscale.** A 400×300 image renders at full column width (~640px), visibly soft. No `max-width` at natural size.
- **Legal pages are genuinely good** — thorough, plain-spoken, SMS/CTIA language present. The only nit is that the contact address is a personal Gmail (`nicholas.aaron.brady@gmail.com`) in both documents.

## What worked well

Worth recording, because these are the parts that felt finished:

- **Audience scoping is correct.** Linda saw the public note and the family milestone; the parents-only photo was invisible to her. No leaks in either direction.
- **Live sync is genuinely good.** The backdated event reached Linda's open page instantly and sorted into the right chronological slot.
- **Inline name capture before a first comment** is a lovely touch — no profile setup, just "what should we call you?" at the moment it matters.
- **Sealed guesses** display as 🎈 placeholders to everyone but their author, and the parent's own guess stays visible. The mechanic is well thought through, right up to the scoring.
- **The anonymous teaser** (blurred timeline + "Sign in to follow along") is the right shape for a forwarded link — it just needs #12 fixed.
- **Slug availability checking and the live theme preview** in setup make the first screen feel responsive and safe.
- **The keepsake artwork is beautiful** — the radial labor clock with the photo inset is a genuinely compelling product. That's exactly why #2 matters.

## Suggested order of attack

1. **#1 + #2** — born-time edits corrupt the record and the printed product. Both are one edit away, both are in the code just merged.
2. **#3** — bound `occurred_at` server-side and add `max` to the Edit modal input.
3. **#12** — the red "Reconnecting…" on every cold visit is cheap to fix and it's the first impression.
4. **#5 + #6 + #7 + #4** — the pool's whole payoff moment. Currently the invited-during-labor viewer can't fully play and loses on a technicality.
5. **#9 + #13** — audience-scope correction after posting, and copy that matches what the app actually does.
6. Everything in Low as polish, with **#14** (theme leakage) the most visible.
