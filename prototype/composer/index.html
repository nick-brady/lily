Composer explorations

Four takes on the compose row and its relationship to the timeline. Open each and compare.

What's wrong with the current one

  • It's a type picker, not a composer. You have to choose a medium before you've had a thought. Six equal-weight buttons means a decision every single time.
  • It floats. A separate card with a gap above and below the timeline it writes into — so it reads as unrelated to the story, when it's the thing that makes the story.
  • The categories overlap. "Milestone" is a meta-type that can also be a photo or a note. "Photo" and "Video" are the same gesture on a phone.
  • Media type is an implementation detail. A parent thinks "I want to share this," not "I want to invoke Video mode."
Option 1

One box

The Facebook lesson. A single open text field — "Share something with luke's family…" — with attachments as quiet secondary icons. Type first, attach second. Joined to the timeline with no gap.

Option 2

Timeline head

The composer is the first row of the timeline card — same white surface, sitting directly above TODAY. What you write appears immediately beneath it. The tightest possible answer to "disassociated from the timeline."

Option 3

Dock

A persistent bottom bar, like every messaging app. The timeline owns the whole page. Always reachable one-handed — which is the actual posture of someone in labor.

Option 4

Right now

Stage-aware. The page offers what this moment calls for and hides the rest behind a +. Shown in both states — mid-labor and just-after-birth — so you can see it change character.
